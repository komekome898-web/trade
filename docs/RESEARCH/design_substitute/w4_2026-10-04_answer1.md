# Answers

Fairness of per-day profit when the variant changes the trade count: per-day total profit on the same data is the fair net comparison, because both runs use the same capital and calendar. It cannot show where a change came from. Report the trade count alongside it. Use per-trade measures only on trades present in both runs.

## Unit 1
**Q1 (ranked)**
1. (c) direct: avoiding big losses is the second half of the purpose, measured by the sum of trades losing more than 10 bp.
2. (b) direct: amplifying small wins is the first half, measured by the sum of winning trades.
3. (h) direct: the owner wants both improvements combined, and this is the combination effect.
4. (g) proxy: average win size reflects amplification but depends on which trades survive.
5. (a) proxy: net result mixes both effects and cannot say which one worked.
6. (d) proxy: breakout closes overlap with big losses but are not defined by loss size.
7. (e) unrelated: trade count is a mechanism or side effect, not an outcome of either aim.
8. (f) unrelated: win rate says nothing about win size or loss size.

**Q2**
1. Trades per day: report. The variant changes it by design, so it cannot be matched, but it explains the profit differences.
2. Fill assumption inside a 1-minute bar: match. Run both arms under the same assumption and show both optimistic and pessimistic.
3. Year: report. Show per-year results to check that the effect is not driven by one year.
4. Share of trades touching an ambiguous bar: report. It bounds how much the result depends on the fill assumption.

## Unit 2
**Q1 (ranked)**
1. (f) direct: loss per breakout-closed trade is exactly the target.
2. (a) direct: the profit of trades closed at a breakout or by the new rule is the targeted group, but the group differs between arms.
3. (b) proxy: big losses mostly come from breakouts but are not the same set.
4. (c) proxy: total profit includes everything unrelated to breakout losses.
5. (d) proxy: the width filter removes entries, so the count shows the mechanism, not the loss reduction.
6. (e) unrelated: winning trades are not the stated target.

**Q2**
1. Trades per day: report. The width filter removes trades by design, and per-day totals are the fair comparison.
2. Fill assumption: match. The close timing of losing positions depends on it.
3. Year: report. Show per-year results to detect regime dependence.
4. Threshold chosen in-sample: report. It cannot be matched. Flag it as optimistic and show results on years not used to choose it.

## Unit 3
**Q1 (ranked)**
1. (a) direct: the entry-only gap is the entry part.
2. (b) direct: the exit-only gap is the exit part.
3. (c) direct: the interaction is the remainder that makes the parts add up to the total gap.
4. (e) direct: reference trades the limit version missed are the entry loss from non-fills.
5. (f) direct: the per-trade difference on common trades isolates the fill-price effect.
6. (d) proxy: the trade-count difference shows the non-fill mechanism but not the profit size.

**Q2**
1. Fill assumption inside a 1-minute bar: match. Limit-fill outcomes depend on it, so show a sensitivity run.
2. Number of trades: report. Missed trades are the mechanism, so split profit into missed versus common trades.
3. Bar length: match. A different bar changes both the reference and the limit fills.
4. Entry style: match. Keep it fixed in exit-only runs and vary only the entry in entry-only runs.

## Unit 4
**Q1 (ranked)**
1. (c) direct: the holding-time bands show whether long holds lose and whether the exit helps.
2. (d) direct: the profit of trades closed by the new exit is the exit's own effect.
3. (g) direct: the per-trade difference on common trades removes selection effects.
4. (a) direct: it is the net result against no exit, but it hides the source.
5. (b) proxy: the win and loss sums show direction but not which trades changed.
6. (f) proxy: re-entry profit is a side effect of the price exit and can contaminate the exit effect.
7. (e) proxy: the trade count shows the mechanism, not the profit effect.

**Q2**
1. Trades per day: report. Exits free the position for re-entry and new entries, so use (g) and (f) to separate those effects.
2. Year: report. Show per-year results to check stability.
3. Bar length: match. N bars and the volatility measure both depend on it.

## Unit 5
**Q1 (ranked)**
1. (b) direct: years not used to set any threshold give the honest test of a rolling gate.
2. (e) direct: year-by-year sign agreement tests whether the rolling gate is stable.
3. (a) direct: it is the headline difference but includes years that set the threshold.
4. (f) direct: the profit of removed trades shows what the gate actually cuts.
5. (d) proxy: the per-trade difference ignores the fact that the gate removes trades.
6. (c) proxy: the trade count shows the gate's intensity, not its benefit.

**Q2**
1. Trades per day: report. The gate removes trades by design, so per-day totals are fair but must be read with the count.
2. Year: match. Compare the same years across arms, and mark which years set a threshold.
3. Volatility level of the year: report. A rolling threshold lags regime shifts, so the effect depends on volatility.

## Unit 6
**Q1 (ranked)**
1. (c) direct: switching one handling to match the other is a causal test of that difference.
2. (a) direct: counting one-sided trades per handling difference attributes the cause.
3. (d) direct: the per-trade difference on common trades isolates price-handling differences.
4. (b) proxy: the yearly total mixes all causes and does not identify any one.

**Q2**
1. Year: report. Show whether the difference is concentrated in particular years.
2. Bar length: match. A different bar length would itself be a handling difference and would confound the attribution.

Per-day profit is not fair as the main cause metric here. The two simulators differ in which trades exist, so use (a) for missing trades and (d) for common trades.
