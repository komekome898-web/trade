## Unit A: wick-reversal strategy (Katsuo), second round

**Q1. Directness ranking, most direct first**
1. (b) interaction: direct. It tests the stated hypothesis that the effects add up.
2. (a) combined minus baseline per day: direct. It is the measurement of the combined form.
3. (c) combined minus better single: direct. It shows whether combining beats the best single change.
4. (h) shape across N = 6..12: direct. The plan asks for N between 6 and 12; flat neighbours show robustness.
5. (i) inner-join vs no-join difference: direct. The plan names the missing-bar handling explicitly.
6. (e) profit by holding-time band: proxy. It shows where the time exit acts, but not the gate's effect.
7. (d) year-by-year sign agreement: proxy. It is a stability check, not the purpose itself.
8. (g) per-trade profit difference: proxy. Gate and exit change the trade mix, so it is confounded.
9. (f) trades per day: proxy. It describes the gate's mechanism, not profit.

**Q2. Covariates**
1. Trades per day: report. The gate lowers it by design; matching it would remove the gate's effect.
2. Year: report. Stratify to see consistency, as in (d); do not match.
3. Bar length: ignore. It is fixed at 15 minutes in every run.
4. Handling of missing Japanese-venue minutes: match. Compare variant and baseline under the same join; (i) reports the contrast.
5. First year with partial gate history: match. Compare on full-history days and also report with them included.
6. Fairness: per-day profit is fair for total yield, with no-trade days counted as zero. It does not separate skill from fewer trades, so report (f) and (g) beside it.

**Q3.** The design does not measure overlap at trade level: whether the gate-removed trades and the time-exit-cut trades are actually different trades. Only that overlap directly tests "different trades". If they overlap heavily, additivity fails for a reason (b) cannot show.

## Unit B: range strategy (Matilda), second round

**Q1. Directness ranking, most direct first**
1. (d) interaction: direct. It tests whether stacking keeps both effects or the two compete.
2. (b) sum of trades losing more than 10 bp: direct. These are the "big losses" the hypothesis names; the 10 bp cutoff needs a stated basis.
3. (e) profit of breakout-closed trades: direct. The gate is hypothesised to act on breakout losses.
4. (g) rolling vs fixed gate: direct. It is the plan's threshold re-derivation question.
5. (a) total profit per day vs baseline: direct. It gives the net outcome but hides which mechanism acted.
6. (c) sum of winning trades: proxy. It shows the cost the take-profit change imposes on winners.
7. (h) year-by-year sign agreement: proxy. It is a stability check, not the purpose.
8. (f) trades per day: proxy. It is a side effect of the gate, not a profit measure.

**Q2. Covariates**
1. Trades per day: report. The gate removes trades by design; matching it would hide the effect.
2. Fill assumption inside a 1-minute bar: match. Compare only within the same assumption, and report both results.
3. Year: report. Stratify to check consistency rather than matching.
4. Threshold chosen in-sample or not: report. This is the contrast in (g), and the in-sample result is optimistic.
5. Days with less than 180 days of history (rolling gate off): match. Compare rolling and fixed gates on the same days.
6. Fairness: per-day profit is fair for total yield only. The take-profit shortens holds and the gate removes trades, so also report the loss and win sums and the trade count.

**Q3.** The design does not measure which individual trades are hit by the gate and which by the take-profit. Without that, "different big losses, so effects stack" cannot be separated from an incidental sum. A second concern is that the optimistic and pessimistic fill results may differ by more than the effects being compared, which would swamp the conclusion.
