## Unit 1: Matilda, combining two improvements

**Q1 (ranked)**
1. (b) Sum of winning trades per day: direct. It measures "amplifying small wins" itself.
2. (c) Sum of trades losing more than 10 bp per day: direct. It measures "avoiding big losses" itself.
3. (h) Interaction term: direct for "combining", not for either viewpoint. It shows whether the two effects add up.
4. (a) Total profit per day: proxy. It is the net of both viewpoints and hides which one moved.
5. (d) Profit of breakout-closed trades: proxy. It covers only one kind of big loss.
6. (g) Average win size: proxy. It shifts with trade selection, not only with win amplification.
7. (f) Win rate: unrelated. It ignores trade size, which is what both viewpoints are about.
8. (e) Number of trades: unrelated. It is a mechanism or covariate, not an outcome.

**Q2 (covariates)**
1. Number of trades per day: report.
   - Per-day profit is fair as a total outcome, because time and capital are fixed and added or removed trades are part of the effect.
   - It cannot show per-trade quality. Report trades per day and a per-trade figure next to it.
2. Fill assumption inside a 1-minute bar: match. Run baseline and variant under the same assumption, and show both optimistic and pessimistic.
3. Year: report. Show the per-year sign so one year cannot carry the result.
4. Share of trades touching an ambiguous bar: report. Stop and exit placement change it between variants and can bias the result.

## Unit 2: Matilda, reducing breakout losses directly

**Q1 (ranked)**
1. (a) Profit of trades closed at a breakout or by the new close rule: direct. It is the loss the owner wants reduced.
2. (f) Loss per breakout-closed trade: direct. It shows the size of the loss, but the trade mix changes when the entry filter removes trades.
3. (b) Sum of trades losing more than 10 bp: proxy. It covers big losses generally, not only breakout ones.
4. (c) Total profit per day: proxy. It is the net outcome, including side effects.
5. (e) Sum of winning trades: proxy. It is only a check that closing earlier does not cut winners.
6. (d) Number of trades: unrelated. It is a covariate.

**Q2 (covariates)**
1. Number of trades per day: report.
   - The "do not enter" filter removes trades, so per-day profit mixes lower exposure with better quality.
   - Per-day total is still the fair headline. Show trades per day and per-trade alongside.
2. Fill assumption: match. Early or late closes are sensitive to intra-bar order.
3. Year: report. Show per-year sign agreement.
4. Threshold chosen in-sample: report as in-sample only. It inflates the gain, so prefer a pre-set threshold or an out-of-sample check.

## Unit 3: Katsuo, why limit orders do worse than the bar-close reference

**Q1 (ranked)**
1. (a) Per-day difference, entry-only limit runs: direct. It is the entry part of the gap.
2. (b) Per-day difference, exit-only limit runs: direct. It is the exit part of the gap.
3. (c) Interaction term: direct. It is the remainder that closes the split.
4. (f) Per-trade difference on trades present in both: proxy. It isolates the fill-price effect, not the missed trades.
5. (e) Profit of reference trades the limit version missed: proxy. It isolates the non-fill sub-part of the entry effect.
6. (d) Per-day difference in number of trades: proxy. It shows the missed-fill mechanism, not the profit gap.

**Q2 (covariates)**
1. Fill assumption inside a 1-minute bar: match. It drives whether a limit fills, so it must be identical across all runs.
2. Number of trades: report. Fewer fills is the main mechanism, so per-day profit is not comparable without it.
   - Per-day profit differences are fair for the total gap but not for the cause.
   - Split the gap into missed trades and price difference on common trades.
3. Bar length: match. It must be the same in every run, or the split is confounded.
4. Entry style: match. Hold it fixed except for the one factor being switched.

## Unit 4: Katsuo, exits for long holds

**Q1 (ranked)**
1. (a) Per-day profit difference vs no-exit: direct. It is the net effect of the exit.
2. (b) Per-day difference of winning and losing sums: direct. The premise is that losses shrink and wins stay.
3. (g) Per-trade difference on trades present in both runs: proxy. It is a clean paired comparison, but it excludes re-entries.
4. (d) Profit of trades closed by the new exit: proxy. It has no counterfactual of what those trades would have earned without the exit.
5. (c) Profit by holding-time band: proxy. It checks the premise, not the exit's effect.
6. (f) Profit of trades re-entered after a price exit: proxy. It is a leakage check.
7. (e) Number of trades per day: unrelated. It is a covariate.

**Q2 (covariates)**
1. Number of trades per day: report.
   - Price exits with re-entry add trades.
   - Per-day total profit is fair because it includes re-entries, but report trades per day and (f) so the gain is not just extra trades.
2. Year: report. Show per-year sign agreement.
3. Bar length: match. N bars means different durations at different bar lengths.

## Unit 5: Katsuo, volatility gate with a rolling threshold

**Q1 (ranked)**
1. (b) Per-day difference restricted to years not used to set any threshold: direct. This is the check on a threshold fixed from the same data.
2. (a) Per-day difference, gated minus ungated: direct. It is the gate's total effect under the rolling threshold.
3. (e) Year-by-year sign agreement: direct as a stability check on whether the threshold is robust.
4. (f) Profit of trades removed by the gate: proxy. It shows what was removed but not the net effect.
5. (d) Per-trade difference: proxy. It separates quality from exposure.
6. (c) Number of trades per day: unrelated. It is a covariate.

**Q2 (covariates)**
1. Number of trades per day: report.
   - The gate removes trades, so per-day profit mixes less exposure with better selection.
   - Per-day total is the fair headline. Show trades per day and per-trade alongside.
2. Year: report. The rolling threshold has no history in the first year, and each year uses a different threshold.
3. Volatility level of the year: report. A threshold from the prior year lags regime changes, so it may gate too much or too little.

## Unit 6: cause of the difference between two simulators

**Q1 (ranked)**
1. (c) Profit difference after switching one handling to match the other: direct. It is a one-factor causal test.
2. (a) Number of trades present in only one simulator, attributed to each handling difference: direct. It attributes trade-set differences to causes.
3. (d) Per-trade difference on common trades: direct. It shows how a handling difference changes the price on the same trade.
4. (b) Yearly profit difference: proxy. It shows the size of the gap, not its cause.

**Q2 (covariates)**
1. Year: report. A handling difference may matter only in some years.
2. Bar length: match. It must be identical in both simulators, or it becomes a cause itself.
3. Per-day profit comparison is not fair here, because the trade sets differ. Compare on common trades per trade, and report the one-sided trades separately.
