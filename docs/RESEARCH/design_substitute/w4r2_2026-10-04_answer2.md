## Unit A: wick-reversal strategy (Katsuo)

**Q1. Ranking of candidate observables:**

(a) per-day profit difference vs baseline: **DIRECT** - directly measures combined effect against stated baseline purpose.

(b) interaction (combined minus sum of singles): **DIRECT** - directly tests the hypothesis that effects add up additively.

(c) per-day profit combined minus better single: **PROXY** - measures superiority but doesn't test additive hypothesis.

(d) year-by-year sign agreement: **PROXY** - shows consistency across years but not the effect magnitude.

(e) profit by holding-time band: **PROXY** - shows mechanism (time exit cuts long losses) but not combined effect directly.

(f) number of trades per day: **UNRELATED** - confound variable, not an observable of strategy performance.

(g) per-trade profit difference: **PROXY** - per-trade effect doesn't account for daily frequency differences.

(h) effect shape across N = 6..12: **PROXY** - optimization curve useful for tuning, not direct effect measurement.

(i) combined difference with/without inner join: **DIRECT** - directly tests the specified mechanism for missing Japanese venue minutes.

**Q2. Candidate covariates:**

- number of trades per day: **MATCH** - variant may reduce/increase trade frequency, directly affects daily profit.
- year: **MATCH** - captures market regime and seasonal patterns.
- bar length: **REPORT** - design specifies 15-minute bars; report if this varies across runs.
- how missing Japanese-venue minutes are handled: **MATCH** - directly tested in design via inner join runs.
- first year with less than 365-day history: **MATCH** - affects gate calculation reliability and comparability.

**Per-day profit fairness:** No, comparing per-day profit is **not fair** when the variant changes trade frequency per day. Strategy A might show higher daily profit solely from more trades, not better per-trade quality. Normalize by trade count or use per-trade metrics.

**Q3. Unmeasured factor that could change conclusion:**

The distribution of profit across winning vs losing days. Same daily profit could come from frequent small wins or rare large wins with different variance and drawdown profiles, affecting practical trading performance.

## Unit B: range strategy (Matilda)

**Q1. Ranking of candidate observables:**

(a) per-day total profit difference vs baseline: **DIRECT** - directly measures combined stacking effect.

(b) per-day sum of trades losing more than 10 bp: **DIRECT** - directly tests hypothesis about big-loss removal.

(c) per-day sum of winning trades: **PROXY** - winning trades matter but don't test loss-removal hypothesis.

(d) interaction (stacked minus sum of singles): **DIRECT** - tests whether effects add up as hypothesized.

(e) profit of breakout trades per day: **PROXY** - related to mechanism but doesn't directly measure loss removal.

(f) number of trades per day: **UNRELATED** - confound variable like Unit A.

(g) rolling-gate vs fixed-gate comparison: **PROXY** - compares gate methods indirectly on stacking hypothesis.

(h) year-by-year sign agreement: **PROXY** - directional consistency, not effect magnitude.

**Q2. Candidate covariates:**

- number of trades per day: **MATCH** - variant may change trade frequency, directly affects daily totals.
- fill assumption (optimistic/pessimistic): **MATCH** - directly tested in design; affects profit calculation.
- year: **MATCH** - temporal patterns and market regimes vary.
- whether threshold chosen in-sample: **MATCH** - in-sample bias is critical confounder.
- days with rolling gate < 180 days history: **MATCH** - affects gate validity and comparability.

**Per-day profit fairness:** No, NOT fair when variant changes trade frequency per day. Normalize by trade count or report per-trade metrics alongside daily totals.

**Q3. Unmeasured factor that could change conclusion:**

Whether the ratio gate and take-profit center actually operate on the same trade population. If they remove losses from completely different trades, stacking shows cumulative benefit, not whether they compete for the same losses as hypothesized.
