**Q1. Observables ranked by directness:**

1. **(c) gate's take (profit avoided per day)** – DIRECT. Directly measures profit gained by applying the purpose operationally.

2. **(a) per-class mean daily profit with 95% interval** – DIRECT. Directly shows which classes lose/barely gain; core evidence for the purpose.

3. **(b) high-minus-low difference with interval** – DIRECT. Directly quantifies whether volatility sorting separates profitable from unprofitable.

4. **(e) per-class number of days** – PROXY. Affects confidence interval width and power, but not whether trades lose or barely gain.

5. **(d) minimum detectable effect per class** – PROXY. Indicates statistical power to detect losses; not actual losses or their avoidance.

6. **(f) per-class share of total profit** – PROXY. Shows profit distribution but not whether individual classes lose or barely gain.

7. **(g) same split applied to pre-improvement version** – UNRELATED. Baseline context; purpose requires measuring the *improved* version's selectivity.

**Q2. Fairness of decision rule:**

No. The rule "avoid if 95% CI upper end ≤ 0" only acts when confident of losses. "Avoid trades that lose or barely gain" suggests avoiding when profitability is uncertain. Fairer rule: avoid if mean ≤ small positive threshold (e.g., 0.1 bp) OR if the 95% CI includes zero, capturing marginal trades.

**Q3. Covariates:**

1. **Fill assumption inside a 1-minute bar** – REPORT. Directly affects slippage and profit calculations; must state optimistic/pessimistic choice.

2. **Calendar year** – REPORT. Thresholds from prior year; volatility regimes shift seasonally and cyclically.

3. **Days without a class (first year)** – REPORT. Excludes data; affects sample representativeness and potential bias.

4. **Whether thresholds chosen in-sample** – REPORT. Critical for look-ahead bias disclosure; if in-sample, validation requires out-of-sample testing.
