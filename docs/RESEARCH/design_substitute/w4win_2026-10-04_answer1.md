# Answer 1 (independent lower model, saw only w4win_2026-10-04_questions.md; verbatim)

**Q1 (ranked by directness)**

1. (a) Direct. It is the improved-minus-baseline effect on unseen days, and the minimum detectable effect shows whether a null result means anything.
2. (c) Direct, for the day rule only. It isolates that rule's marginal effect, and the rule was chosen on old data.
3. (g) Direct, for M only. The 120–165 bp/day fill gap probably exceeds the effect, so conclusions depend on the pessimistic result.
4. (b) Direct but low power. The product changed on 2024-03-28, so the split shows whether the effect survives. Each half has fewer days.
5. (d) Proxy. Sign agreement discards magnitude and noise. A sign can match by chance, so it cannot confirm carry-over.
6. (f) Proxy. Calendar-year slices are underpowered and confound regime with the product change. They are useful only as a consistency check.
7. (e) Proxy. Per-trade profit and trades per day describe mechanism, not net profit. Changes in the two can offset each other.

**Q2**

1. Product period: match. Baseline and improved forms must be compared on identical days, and the split is also reported.
2. Fill assumption (M): match. Compare baseline and M-A under the same assumption, and report both optimistic and pessimistic results.
3. Number of settings selected from: report. K-A and K-B were best of 7 and the day rule came from old data, so old-data effects are inflated.
4. Calendar year: report. It affects interpretation but is not a controllable pairing variable, and power per year is low.
5. Volatility level of the window vs the old data: report. The gates and the day rule are volatility-based, so a different level changes how often they trigger.

**Q3**

1. M's real limit-order fills (queue position, adverse selection) are not measured. True results may fall outside the optimistic–pessimistic bounds, and the gap exceeds the plausible effect.
